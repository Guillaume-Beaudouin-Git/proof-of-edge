# The lesson registry

One numbered verdict per lesson, with the number that earned it. Lessons are engraved after a
study closes, never edited afterwards; when a later study corrects one, the correction is
appended and cross-referenced. Numbers below L11 and the gaps (L16, L40–L41, L51–L60) belong to
the private part of the programme or were never engraved.

Each lesson ends with the study that produced it, in parentheses.

### Crypto — structural

L11. Long-only cross-sectional crypto is β-dominated (portfolio correlation to BTC ≥ 0.80 regardless of information coefficient). Only escape: delta-neutral.
L11b. The IJR cost gate is misapplied to binary take-profit/stop-loss strategies (breakout, event-driven): gross ∝ √N, cost ∝ N → gate ratio = C/√N, *decreasing* in N (IC = 0.05 plus FX intraday costs: passes only at N ≤ 6 trades/yr) — a miscalibration, not an infeasibility of the class. Correct binary TP/SL gate: E[gross/trade] > 3 × cost/trade (N-independent). The IC must be pre-registered from the numerical literature (win rate by regime/filter), never adjusted after a failure. (First case: Asian range breakout, archived 2026-05-26.)
L12. Event-count ceiling gate: the pre-flight computes [N_floor, N_ceiling]. At 0.17 % round-trip, the ceiling is ≈ 29 events/yr per position for a 5 %/yr net edge.
L12b. HAC deflation under autocorrelation: persistent regime signals (half-life > 30 d) have N_eff ≪ N_nominal. ETH net-flow: naive t = 2.58 → HAC t = 0.11 (N_eff ≈ 73 out of 3,398).
L13. Variance-risk-premium unit error: the academic VRP is in variance units, not % of notional. Black–Scholes conversion: BTC gross ≈ 4.4 %/yr against 8.9 %/yr of costs → net −4.46 %/yr.
L14. A testnet Sharpe is inadmissible (N < 250). SR 9.64 on testnet → proxy SR 2.0 for the portfolio simulation.
L15. Regime overlays on a high-Sharpe sleeve (SR > 1.0) give a sub-threshold lift (≈ 1.7 %/yr, Δ = +0.022 %/day). Only useful when MaxDD > 20 % (condition corrected by L54).
L17. Fear-and-greed velocity divergence is a momentum proxy with the wrong sign. Existing-holder sentiment branch closed.
L18. Wikipedia attention velocity: zero IC, momentum-proxy direction. New-buyer attention branch closed. Both pathways of the Da et al. taxonomy exhausted.

### FX — structural

L23. Regulatory-window kill: an edge that depends on a fixing or auction window later widened or restructured by regulators (BIS WMR, Feb 2015: 1 min → 5 min; MiFID II, 2018) has pre-reform magnitudes that do not predict post-reform ones. Pre-flight: does the cited paper cover ≥ 2 years of post-change data? If not, non-tradable until re-documented on a post-reform sample.
L23b. Institutional ≠ retail microstructure edge: the persistence of an HFT-mediated edge in the literature says nothing about retail tradability. Any HFT-mediated edge gets an explicit cost-tier check (institutional 0.5–1.5 bp RT vs retail 2–7 bp RT). Default sceptical.

### Prop firms — structural

L19. NQ market impact for a prop-firm account: at-market entry at 09:46 ET, baseline slippage ≈ 4 ticks RT (\$2/MNQ); adverse fills (gap opens, thin pre-market) up to 3×. Asymmetric — losing trades near the stop suffer most, with no room to recover. Stress ≥ 1.5× (3× conservative) in the sizing analysis.
L20. The Deflated Sharpe Ratio is mandatory from the second modification onwards. Bailey–López de Prado: SR_DSR = SR × correction(T, N_trials, ρ). Example: 10 modifications, T = 756 days → SR_DSR ≈ 1.0–1.1 from a reported 1.52. Apply before citing a Sharpe anywhere.
L21. Sharpe reality check by category (live, after costs, after DSR): cross-sectional academic daily (N ≥ 100) 0.3–1.2; single-instrument live day-trade (NQ ORB class) 0.8–2.0; delta-neutral crypto carry (mainnet, N < 500) 1.5–4.0 with high estimation uncertainty. A backtest SR more than 2× its category range triggers a mandatory DSR / cherry-pick audit before being cited.
L22. A Monte-Carlo simulation of the prop-firm evaluation is mandatory before any purchase. (a) The canonical configuration matters — simulate both phases separately; (b) a daily consistency cap can bind before the drawdown does; (c) FTMO does not support CME futures (confirmed 2026); (d) a Gaussian generator overestimates the bust probability of stop-capped strategies — use a calibrated trade-based generator. Tool: `core/risk/trailing_dd_simulator.py`.
L24. The probability of passing a prop-firm challenge is driven by the *shape* of the daily-return distribution against a *symmetric* barrier (target ≈ max drawdown), not by account size — it is scale-invariant (the NQ strategy plateaus at ≈ 44 % p(pass) from \$10k to \$200k). Lumpy strategies (low win rate, high win/loss ratio) are structurally capped at ≈ 44–54 % whatever the sizing: the wrong tool for a symmetric-barrier challenge. Challenge-suitable: high *daily* Sharpe, win rate > 55 %, win/loss ≈ 1, low kurtosis, high trade frequency (the edge must materialise within ≈ 40 trading days before variance dominates). Momentum and trend are inherently lumpy → poor fit. Mean reversion fits better but hits the L11/L12 cost ceiling and left-tail kurtosis caps.

### CFD / prop-firm execution and structure (engraved 2026-06-10)

L25. Two-sided test: a real directional edge is positive on *both* legs. Every directional family tested on the CFD venue was long-leg-only, i.e. the equity/carry up-drift, i.e. beta. Mandatory gate on any directional claim.
L26. Delay-1 / same-bar discipline: overlapping windows and same-bar fills inflate intraday edges; validate with non-overlapping trades and a one-bar execution-delay test (decisive). Any timestamp ambiguity → next bar.
L27. Market-neutral *by construction* ≠ two-sided *in the spread*: a cointegrated spread inherits a relative drift, so stat-arb still fails L25. The beta trap survives hedging.
L28. A market-neutral CFD pair pays the broker's financing markup twice (the benchmark rate cancels across legs) ≈ 1.5 bps/night in every rate era — fatal to spreads that take weeks to revert.
L29. Half-life-matched holding is necessary but not sufficient: fixing the exit removes a confound so that the true "no" becomes visible; it does not manufacture expected value.
L30. Swap-sign rule: real CFD swaps are *asymmetric* (you pay more than fair on the costly side and receive less than fair on the carry side). Never hold the negative-swap side overnight; a long premium and a short positive swap cannot be collected together. Decisive case: US100 long at −3.31 bps/night turned an overnight harvester from 82 % to 31 % capture.
L31. Carry and short-term-reversal alpha decay: edges that are real *in their regime* (JPY carry 2022–24, US-index short-term reversal pre-2013, EURGBP 2022–23) decay out of it; an out-of-sample / post-regime split is mandatory before citing any carry or reversal edge; unwind tails (−15/−20 %) breach a 10 % floor.
L32. Retail-CFD commodity carry is one-sided-uncollectable: the short-contango leg needed for most of history costs −46+ bps/month in swap — "collecting the roll" is structurally unavailable to retail.
L32b. The collectable side is a credit, not an edge: long-only oil gated on real backwardation nets ≈ 0 over 19 years (+0.96 %/yr swap credit cancelled by −1.03 %/yr of conditioned price beta); basis gating protects only against contango-led crashes and rides backwardation-led ones (2008, 2014, 2022). Both CFD carry sides closed.
L33. The real instrument's *cost structure*, not the signal, decides whether a barrier exploit lives: a 2-bp backtest does not transfer to a 6–10 bp CFD; the ORB's mean R flips sign between 2 and 3 bps RT; swap is charged on *held notional* per leg, in both directions. Measure book-level cost; never assume it.
L33b. Spread is the executioner of intraday-flat structures: with zero swap the binding cost is the spread — a time-of-day effect at ≈ 0 gross becomes −3 bps net (t −6) at a conservative 3 bps RT. An intraday-flat signal needs more than 3 bps gross to exist.
L34. Flow-anchored FX is institutional-tier only: the sole gross survivor at fix/session level (two-sided, t = 3.55, gross 2.07 bps/day) dies solely on the retail cost tier (needs < ≈ 1 bp RT per leg against 2.48 measured). Pre-2015-reform fix magnitudes remain non-tradable (L23). Reformulated 2026-06-10 without the asymmetry clause, which was a timezone artifact.
L35. Dynamic-programming-optimal sizing is the *ceiling* of every sizing policy, not a heuristic: the ORB's single-attempt two-step ceiling is 33 % on the full sample. Money management is not the lever — the edge is.
L36. Aggregate overnight carry pre-kills every held premium on a CFD account: book-level all-in carry (≈ 6.75 %/yr swap + ≈ 1.25 % commission) ≈ the entire multi-asset trend premium (≈ 8 %/yr) → net Sharpe 0.0. The killer is the *aggregate* multi-leg carry (low-volatility, large-notional legs dominate), not the single-instrument rate. Carry-immune escapes only: intraday-flat (then L33b binds) or premium ≫ carry.

### Trend, book and FX intraday

L37. Filter sanity check: for any conditionally filtered sleeve, count the fraction of time where sign(filter) ≠ native signal. Below 5 %, the filter is cosmetic — name the sleeve by what it really is (a "carry-aligned trend" on USDJPY was long-only TSMOM).
L38. Beta-orthogonality test: regress any "structural premium" sleeve on a vol-targeted buy-and-hold of its primary asset; report (β, HAC α, R²). R² > 30 % ⇒ not orthogonal — it is timing on beta; present it as such.
L39. Daily versus monthly skew dissociation: on trend and carry, daily skew can be negative while monthly skew is positive. Daily skew binds on a 5 % daily barrier; monthly skew on a personal book. Always report both.
L42. Pre-FOMC drift is real on equity indices (confirmed on 8 instruments); post-event *reversal* is the wrong direction — post-event momentum continues (25 of 142 AMTP-significant cells are negative-reversal).
L43. Retail FX intraday-flat microstructure is cost-dead across reversal, drift and seasonality: exploitable gross is sub-bp against ≈ 2–5 bps CFD RT (2007–2026, 10 majors, true UTC, three orthogonal angles). Generalises L34 from fix windows to the whole retail FX intraday-flat space. Pre-flight: gross per trade / p95 cost ≥ 1.5 or dead — best observed 0.36.
L44. A mechanism-driven cross-sectional contrast is the cheap, decisive IC falsifier: split the universe by where the mechanism should and should not apply, pre-register the contrast pool — a weak pooled t became a clean verdict (European t 2.11 vs Asia-native −0.57: the signal is real, death is by cost).

### Renumbered and new — reconciliation of 2026-06-10

L45. Two-sided crypto-CFD TSMOM is structurally infeasible on a CFD account (three-axis kill): the short leg is not significant even at a zero-swap counterfactual (BTC t 0.28, N = 205); the long leg of any two-sided crypto TSMOM ≡ long-only (the clip makes it L11 β-long in disguise); 7-night holds × crypto swap ≈ 19 bps/trade eat the rest. Weekly crypto momentum closed on CFD; exchange-native or intraday-flat only.
L46. Prop-firm honesty checklist: one canonical ledger, a measured real cost, a frozen spec, a full-sample regime. When all four are present, sign-flips between reports disappear — every historical ORB sign-flip was a cost-hypothesis difference on the same ledger.
L47. Annualise by √(observations per year), not √252: a once-a-week edge annualises in √52 — √252 inflates its Sharpe by ×2.2 (the Monday-sleeve case).
L48. A registry check must sweep consolidated paths: a sweep that greps only historical paths after a consolidation returns a false "file deleted" and nearly downgrades a STOP into a caveat. Every avoided trial at high N protects the DSR of all future candidates.
L49. Token-unlock drift is short-alt-beta: a calendar-event claim on a structurally bleeding universe must clear a *matched placebo calendar*, not just significance, decay and DSR gates — the unlock short shows net +337 bps/event, t 2.90, N = 578, undecayed, DSR 0.94, and still sits *below* the placebo median of random vol-matched days on the same tokens (p95 +539). Flat dose–response, zero insider gradient and a linear event-time escalator complete the falsification. (`studies/crypto_flows_placebo`.)
L50. Funding extremes confirm momentum across exchange and cycle: an earlier finding (Hyperliquid, z-score, 2023–26) replicated independently (Binance, trailing one-year percentile, 2020–26, 13 perps, 2,195 events) — extreme-high funding precedes *continuation* (+20/+30/+113 bps at 8 h/24 h/72 h; +239 at 72 h in 2020–21). The funding-contrarian family is closed at the exchange-perp tier; funding-percentile carry-sizing overlays have no IC foundation.

### August–September 2026

L61. Path-dependent sizing fabricates a positive t-statistic out of pure noise, and HAC does not see it. Staggered (pyramided) entry — add at 1σ, 2σ, 3σ — on intraday mean reversion produced 18/18 cells above t 2.5, mean +8.61, gross 2.91 bps/trade (above the 1.4 bps cost floor) and net t +4.81: it would have cleared every gate and been deployed. A placebo of matched construction reproduces t +5.8 to +14.7 on pure noise; the single-shot entry on the *same* placebo produces nothing (t ≈ −1 to +1), so the placebo is sound and the pyramid is the artifact. Mechanism: staggering makes position size correlated with the path already travelled, so the P&L series inherits autocorrelation *by construction* — it lives in the sizing, not the residual, which is why HAC lag-6 cannot correct it. ⇒ Any path-dependent sizing (pyramid, martingale, anti-martingale, drawdown scaling) requires a placebo of *identical* construction; a bare t means nothing there.
L62. A block-bootstrap placebo fabricates a bar geometry that does not exist — check the artifact's own artifact. The shared engine's pessimistic intrabar tie-break (a bar touching both stop and target is scored as the stop) is real and monotone in bracket width (1×ATR brackets → mean t −1.96; time-stop only → −0.003). But its magnitude on a block-bootstrap placebo (12–18 % ambiguous bars, −3 to −4 bps/trade) is ten times its magnitude on real data (1.6 %, −0.28 to −0.37 bps). Correcting it is worth 0.8–1.2 t, not 4–5. Result: 13 studies audited, zero verdicts reversed. ⇒ Never compare a bracket strategy to a naive block-bootstrap placebo — the placebo's intrabar structure is not the market's.
L63. A borrowed measurement is admissible when the margin to the decision threshold exceeds the plausible error of the source; it is not when they are of the same order. The ORB was killed for weeks on a spread borrowed from another broker (1.80 bps) where the venue-native measure is 0.96–1.05 — fatal because the frontier sat at 0.59–1.94 bps, a margin of ≈ ×1. The same borrowed source killed the energy family at 312/79/72 bps against a gross ceiling of 1.2 bps — margins of ×179 to ×780, out of reach of any plausible source error, so that verdict stands. ⇒ State the provenance every time ("measured on X"), and state the margin.
L64. **A time split chosen after seeing the data produces a break that disappears as soon as the date is left free — and correcting for the search costs most of the t.** Twice in the same session, on two independent sleeves. (a) ORB — the short leg seems to "turn positive in 2022–2026" (+0.142R, beating the long); an *endogenous* break search (Bai–Perron, free date) finds a break on 2021-11-26, suspiciously close to the hand-picked split, |t| = 2.29 — but once corrected by block bootstrap over the ≈ 1,486 candidate dates swept, it is the 73rd percentile of pure noise (p = 0.266); max CUSUM 1.07 < 1.36, no instability. Rolling windows: the signal never exceeds t = 1.8 on any two-year window in 16 years. (b) Overnight — Monday seems to "have collapsed since 2022" (t 2.08 → 0.73 on US100, 2.28 → 0.02 on GER40); same test, p = 0.43 to 0.61 in all four cells: indistinguishable from noise. ⇒ **Every regime break must be *estimated*, never imposed, and its significance corrected for the number of dates swept.** Operational corollary: the right test is the one on *the object actually deployed* — Monday + Tuesday *pooled* as one hypothesis gives t = 4.65 (US100, above the cumulative threshold of 4.21) where each day alone caps at 2.29. Splitting a deployed object into its components multiplies the tests and destroys power without teaching anything.
L65. **The minimum detectable effect is computed with *power*, not with the significance threshold — forgetting it makes an underpowered result look comfortable.** Naive MDE `2.0·σ/√n` (significance only) versus the correct MDE `(z_{1−α/2} + z_{1−β})·σ/√n = 2.80·σ/√n` at 80 % power: a factor of 1.40. Measured on the ORB 2022–2026 (n = 1,115, net at 2.11 bps): effect +0.0834R, naive MDE 0.0740 (ratio 1.13, "it passes") versus MDE at 80 % power 0.1036 (ratio 0.80, the effect is *below* its own floor). The verdict flips. ⇒ Always report the MDE at 80 % power and the effect/MDE ratio; a ratio below 1 means *underpowered*, not "almost significant". (A mistake made and corrected in the same session.)
L66. **Resampling several separately quoted instruments onto a common timestamp *fabricates* cross-predictability — and no *temporal* placebo catches it.** Measured on FX cross-rate coherence (the gap between a directly quoted cross and its synthetic replication through two majors): a pre-registered grid of 18 cells, 18/18 above the maximum under H0 (2.40), 17/18 at |t| > 2.5, t down to −32.3, all of the same sign; a circular-shift placebo passed (real t at the 98.5–100th percentiles of a null that was itself well calibrated); 4/4 walk-forward folds; tight bootstrap CI; effect/MDE ratio 5.2. A textbook PASS — and an artifact through and through. Cause: the "last print" of three instruments has different ages within the same bin, so the gap measures *differential quote staleness*, not information. Three diagnostics settle it, none of them temporal: (a) *jump test* — shifting by one more bar collapses the effect (fraction retained 6 % / 46 % / 23 %); economic information dissipates gradually, a synchronisation defect vanishes at the next bar; (b) *leg decomposition* — the gap also predicts the two other legs, with t of the same order and *opposite sign* (cross −7.23 vs majors +14.91 / +11.23): a mechanism that does not "know" which of the three legs is stale; (c) *TWAP reconstruction* instead of last print — the strongest triangle of the grid (t −10.68) falls to +0.51, sign reversed; the two others lose 50–60 %. ⇒ **As soon as a hypothesis links several separately quoted instruments, a temporal placebo (shift, permutation, bootstrap) is insufficient: it preserves the staleness structure it is supposed to test.** Require the three diagnostics above before any validation. Related family: Epps effect, non-synchronous trading bias (Scholes–Williams 1977, Lo–MacKinlay 1990). A *mechanical* kill (construction artifact) ⇒ not reopenable.
L67. **In Grinold's `IR = TC × IC × √BR`, BR is the number of *independent bets* per year — not the number of *observations*. Confusing the two is the most profitable mistake of the framework, and it goes unnoticed because the formula stays dimensionally correct.** Measured on a third-party macro FX score model: announced BR 1,755 (= the number of trading days over ≈ 7 years) against 0.67 actionable trades per year measured on the same specification once the signal threshold is applied — a factor of 2,619× on BR, 51× on √BR. Combined with a transfer coefficient *assumed* at 0.20 instead of measured (0.50 here), the announced IR of 0.87 falls to 0.042 with all three terms measured. ⇒ **Always measure BR as the number of opportunities the strategy *actually* takes** (after threshold, after filter, after capital constraint), and **measure TC** instead of assuming it — it is the only term that converts predictive power into performance, and the only one nobody checks. Corollary: a high IR on a model with *rare* signals is almost always a breadth/observations confusion; the zero-cost check is to divide BR by the number of years and ask "do I really take that many positions a year?". Two side notes from the same session: (a) when two placebo constructions contradict each other (here a fixed half-life said p = 0.245, "noise"; one matched to the walk-forward procedure said p = 0.0000, "signal"), the contradiction *is* the result — diagnose, never keep the favourable version; the fold-by-fold diagnostic showed the effect lived *entirely in one fold* (2018–2022, COVID), with an in-sample/out-of-sample sign reversal in 3 folds out of 4. (b) `np.isnan()` does not catch `±inf`: a surprise window with zero variance produces an infinite z-score that silently passes the cleaning filter.

## Candidate — named in session, number to be engraved at the next reconciliation

**"Attribution of the kill"** (proposed 2026-09-06). **A death verdict must name *what* dies: the mechanism itself, or only one of its instantiations.** Before archiving anything, enumerate *in writing* the instantiation axes that were *not* varied — universe construction, vehicle, capital, frequency, horizon, estimator, exit geometry, regime — and say for each whether it could plausibly reverse the verdict. As long as one plausible axis remains unvaried, the kill is *partial* and falls into a reopenable category; it can *never* be called "mechanical".

The enumeration is free: it is written, not tested (Δn_tests = 0). Testing those axes is a separate decision with its own budget. That is what makes the rule compatible with the DSR and with the hard rule "at most three modifications": the three-modification budget bounds the retouching done to make a hypothesis *pass* (false-positive risk), this rule bounds the validity domain of a *negative* result (false-negative risk). Opposite directions, no conflict — provided the obligation bears on the writing, never on the testing.

The attribution error cuts both ways, measured twice in the same session: (a) *false negative* — "the macro score is dead" when only *one* configuration was (cross-sectional, held, N ≥ 30, retail vehicle, small capital); breadth, vehicle and capital had not been varied, and the next round surfaced four live axes, one of them a priority. (b) *false positive at reopening* — pre-FOMC drift classed "underpowered, reopenable" (truncated sample, 49 of 102 events) when the *pre*-announcement drift is an uncertainty-resolution premium, hence structurally non-directional (no symmetric leg to test, even in theory) and dead since 2015–2016 (Kurov, Wolfe & Gilbert) ⇒ a *mechanical* kill, stays closed. A rule that catches only case (a) would be half a rule: this one forces naming *which* of the five motives kills, so it catches both.

Anti-box-ticking safeguard: the enumeration is re-read at reopening. An axis listed as "could change the verdict" and then tested without effect is visible — the enumeration calibrates itself over time.
